# E7: magnitude or direction? The value knob's currency, per architecture

**Hypothesis.** Value is salience written in whatever coordinate the architecture
preserves: magnitude where magnitude survives (DRC and ResNet run `IdentityNorm`
and gate rather than normalise), direction where a pre-LayerNorm block divides
each token by its own standard deviation (ViT, route model).

**Verdict: the prediction holds, the proposed mechanism only half holds.** A
direction edit works on three of four architectures, including the ViT where a
gain fails outright. But the reason the ViT's gain fails is *not* that its first
LayerNorm deletes it — that explanation fits the route model and not the ViT.

Tables: `e7-edit-<base>.<family>.txt`, `e7-profile-<base>.txt`.
JSON: `figures/data/gain_vs_factorised/e7/`; deltas stay on the volume.

## The two edits
Both have exactly two parameters, one per colour plane, on the same slice of the
same tensor (the first linear layer's kernel: DRC `conv_list_0`, ResNet
`xXx_Input_xXx`, ViT `embed`, route model `cell_in`).

- **gain** (`GainEdit`, pre-existing): `K_c -> (1 + shift·g_c) K_c`. Same
  direction, new length. Exactly equivalent to scaling colour `c`'s observation plane.
- **rotation** (`RotationEdit`, new): `K_c -> ||K_c||·(u_c + t_c·u_p)/||u_c + t_c·u_p||`,
  turning colour `c`'s slice towards the other colour's. New direction, same
  length to within float32 rounding.

## 1. Does the first normalisation delete a gain?
A LayerNorm discards the part of a perturbation along the ones vector and along
the token itself, keeping only the part that turns it. Shares of the gain's
change at the embedding, and the survival measured by applying the checkpoint's
*own* first LayerNorm:

| | ones | magnitude | direction | survival |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ViT, gain | 0.01 | **0.33** | 0.66 | **0.750** |
| ViT, turn | 0.06 | 0.02 | 0.93 | 0.934 |
| route model, gain | 0.03 | **0.95** | 0.03 | **0.132** |
| route model, turn | 0.02 | 0.00 | 0.98 | 0.810 |

- **The route model behaves as predicted.** Its token is `cell_in(cells)` plus
  small row/column embeddings, so the content term dominates and a colour gain is
  95% a pure length change. The first LayerNorm removes seven eighths of it.
- **The ViT does not.** Its position embedding is comparable to the content term
  (which is why `input_scale` exists at all), so scaling the content moves the
  token *sideways* rather than along itself: only a third of the gain is a length
  change and three quarters of it survives.
- The DRC and the ResNet have no normalisation to pass through (`IdentityNorm`,
  confirmed in `presets.py`); their profiles show survival ≈ 1 stage to stage.

## 2. The edit that works (reach = slope of held-out τ on the trained shifts; 1 = does what it was trained to)

| base | gain reach | rotation reach | gain cos(Ju,Jv) | rotation cos(Ju,Jv) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DRC s1234 | 0.89 (E4, input conv) | **1.03** | 0.94 (E4) | **0.96** |
| ResNet s1234 | 0.48 (min reached 73%) | **0.71** (89%) | 0.56 | **0.91** |
| ResNet s2468 | — | **0.76** | — | **0.89** |
| ViT s9012 | **0.07** | **0.73** | 0.28 | 0.51 |
| route model s1 | 0.09 | 0.12 | 0.01 | 0.13 |
| route model s2 | — | 0.53 | — | 0.21 |

- **The ViT is the decisive case:** same network, same search, same two
  parameters, and a turn does eleven times what a gain does (0.73 against 0.07).
  A 5.8° turn of colour 1's embedding row moves τ from −0.2 to +10.8 with routes
  100% optimal. Both seeds agree to cos +1.000.
- **Direction is the more general currency,** working on the DRC and ResNet too,
  where it matches the fitted axis in function space at least as well as the gain
  does (0.96 and 0.89–0.91 against 0.94 and 0.56) and damages competence less.
- **The route model resists both**, consistent with its axis holding 0.00% of its
  length in `cell_in`: its value knob is not at the input at all. s2 reaches 0.53
  where s1 reaches 0.12, so this is base-dependent, not a clean architecture fact.
- **Rotation floors at τ ≈ 0 on the negative side** (ViT −0.18 at the largest
  dose, DRC 1.55, ResNet 0.25) — turning one colour towards the other can at most
  make them indistinguishable, which means "prefer the nearer objective". Getting
  below parity needs the swap regime the family allows but the search did not pick.

## 3. Which shape is the axis? (R² of the axis's activation change, deepest informative state)

| base | gain | turn | random |
|---|---|---|---|
| DRC s1234 (cell 2) | **0.28** | 0.13 | 0.08 |
| ResNet s1234 (stage 6) | **0.27** | 0.04 | 0.01 |
| ViT s9012 (block 3) | 0.07 | **0.21** | 0.02 |
| route model s1 (any) | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.01 |

The unnormalised networks' axes are gain-shaped, the ViT's is turn-shaped, and
the route model's is neither — it lives in the MLPs.

## Caveats
- **n = 1 for the ViT** (only s9012 has an axis at all; the other ViT bases have
  reliability 0.05–0.13). The ViT's gain-versus-turn contrast, the headline, rests
  on that single base. ResNet has two bases, the route model two, DRC one here.
- **Axis reliability limits the function-space cosines**: 0.29 for the ViT and
  0.12–0.21 for the ResNet, so those numbers are attenuated and 0.51 is a floor,
  not a ceiling.
- **These R² are a lower bound and are not E1's numbers.** E1 regressed on two
  brightness directions fitted per layer; this uses one fixed antisymmetric gain
  and one fixed turn as single predictors.
- **Deliverable 3's states are the reader's**, so the DRC contributes three
  (cells) and not its convolutions.
- The search is Adam, `--lr 3e-2`, two parameters, `--target soft` pinned; seeds
  agree everywhere (0.86–1.00), so non-convergence is not driving any of this.
