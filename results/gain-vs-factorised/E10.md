# E10: do the study's instruments detect a non-gain mechanism?

Chain finished 16:26 UTC 2026-09-17 on rahul-gm-gvf-e10 (deleted). Its agent was
killed by a session restart; assembled by the main session from the chain's
tables. Bases novalue11g1.s1234 and s9012, o1 axis.

E5 (the factorised control network) failed, so nothing yet showed that these
tests would report "not a gain" if the mechanism were not a gain. This runs the
tests on knobs that move tau and are demonstrably not input salience gains:
E4's **late** edit (cell 2 + readout), E4's **readout** edit, E7's **rotation**,
and `--rule contingent`, which shifts tau one way where the rich objective is
above and the other way where it is below -- not a value knob by construction.
Each is rescaled to the axis's own threshold shift before comparison.

## 1. Competence and the split statistic (s1234, base tau 7.20)

`split` = tau(rich above) - tau(rich below); the base's is -1.40. A value knob
keeps it, a position-contingent edit pulls it apart.

| knob | tau + | tau - | reached | on route | split |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base | 7.20 | - | 100% | 95.1% | -1.40 |
| axis | 4.39 | 11.82 | 100% | 94-95% | -0.92 / -0.98 |
| brightness | 4.54 | 13.16 | 100% | 93-96% | -0.72 / -2.57 |
| rotation | 6.49 | 8.33 | 100% | 95% | -1.07 / -1.44 |
| late | 3.92 | 11.36 | ~100% | 94-96% | -0.02 / +1.34 |
| readout | 2.77 | 3.54 | **31%** | 82-88% | **+7.07 / +6.90** |
| contingent | 4.88 | 5.32 | **51-79%** | **70-73%** | -0.08 / +1.07 |

s9012 is the same picture and sharper on the contingent edit: reached 66-74%,
route 64-77%, split **-6.10 / +10.51** against a base of +0.17.

**Power: yes.** The readout and contingent edits cannot even reach the axis's
threshold shift without destroying navigation, and both blow the split statistic
apart. The axis, brightness and rotation leave both intact.

## 2. Gain alignment (R^2 of the activation change on the brightness fingerprint)

At cell 2, tick 2 -- one of the informative sites, since dense and logits funnel
everything:

| knob | R^2 |
|---|---|
| brightness, tau-matched (reference) | 0.62 |
| full axis | 0.43 |
| **late** | **0.004** |
| **contingent** | **0.001** |
| readout | ~0.002 |
| random, module-matched | 0.001 |
| random, input conv matched | 0.000 |
| **rotation** | **0.87** |

**Power: yes, with one exception.** Three non-gain knobs and two random controls
all sit at the noise floor while the axis sits at 0.43 and brightness at 0.62.
So the E1 measurement does discriminate, and E1's DRC result stands.

**The exception matters:** rotation scores 0.87, above the axis itself. That is
expected on reflection -- to first order a rotation is "dim colour 1 a little and
paint colour 0 onto its objects", which lies inside the span of the two colour
contributions that the fingerprint regresses on. So the gain-alignment metric
tests **"in the span of the colour contributions"**, not "a length change
specifically". It cannot separate gain from rotation, and the write-up must not
claim it does. Separating those two is what the edit searches and the
weights-only arm sweep are for.

## 3. Object-removal patching

Fraction of each knob's threshold shift carried by the object-independent and
object-dependent halves of its activation change (cf. E8, where the axis's
object-independent half carried 0-13%):

| knob | site | object-independent | object-dependent |
|---|---|---|---|
| late | cell 2 | 0.15 | 0.80 |
| contingent | cell 2 | -3.97 | 4.22 |

**Power: no, for the late edit.** Its profile looks like the axis's, so this test
does not separate them. The contingent edit's numbers are incoherent (patches
carrying -4x and +8x of the shift), which flags it as a different kind of object
rather than measuring it.

## Verdict

Two of the three instruments discriminate: competence-plus-split cleanly, gain
alignment at the noise floor for every non-gain knob except rotation. The
object-removal test does not discriminate. So the study may claim that its
positive results on the DRC are not what these tests report for arbitrary knobs
that move tau -- with the explicit caveat that gain versus rotation is decided
elsewhere.

This is an instrument control, not the factorised-network control. It says the
tests can tell a salience change from a late edit, a readout edit or a
position-contingent edit. It does not say they would recognise a genuinely
factorised value variable, which still needs E5's replacement.
