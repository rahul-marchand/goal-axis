# E8: the leftover, the lever's ceiling, and censored τ

DRC g1 bases s1234, s9012, s3456e; o1 axis; writes at ±0.30 unless stated; 2,048 held-out
test levels per rollout, 768 valid levels for the teacher-forced sizes.

Tables: `results/gain-vs-factorised/leftover-<base>.o1.txt`, `censored-tau-<base>.txt`.
JSON: `figures/data/leftover/`. Code: `goalmisgen/analysis/leftover.py`,
`scripts/leftover_patch.py`, `scripts/censored_tau.py`, `tests/test_leftover.py` (6 pass).
Run from the frozen snapshot `/workspace/gvf-e8-snap`.

## 1. The leftover is inert: it carries ~4% of the threshold shift

With the colour-1 objective erased, a gain changes nothing, yet the axis still moves
activations (E2). Patching only that object-independent component into the base on ordinary
levels, as a fraction of the write's own threshold shift:

| patched at | s1234 | s9012 | s3456e |
|---|---|---|---|
| cell 2 | 0.04 | 0.01 | 0.13 |
| every site | 0.04 | 0.00 | 0.05 |
| *(object-dependent part, every site)* | *0.96* | *1.02* | *0.98* |
| *(full change, every site — the plumbing check)* | *1.00* | *1.00* | *1.00* |

**The object-independent part is not a second, object-free value bias.** It is noise and
encoder side effects: of the three candidate explanations, the numbers support the first two
and rule out the third.

Supporting sizes (share of the write's activation change surviving erasure, at cell 2 / logits):
axis 0.32/0.16, 0.18/0.08, 0.55/0.48 — against a module-matched **random** direction at
0.99–1.08 everywhere, and the axis's own **gain part** at 0.04/0.01 (the identity
`tests/test_leftover.py` checks). So the axis is far more object-tied than noise is, and what
is left over behaves like the noise.

Caveat: the random control's own threshold shift is small (0.95 / −2.74 / 1.27 steps against
the axis's 7.4 / 8.3 / 5.8), so its *fractions* have a tiny denominator and are unstable
(−3.55 to +1.70); use it as a reference for the activation shares, not for the fractions.

## 2. The ceiling is in the representation, and the two levers are not symmetric

**Matched levers.** Both writes below carry the *same* first-order threshold slope as the
axis's own gain slice — one by dimming colour 0, one by brightening colour 1 — at the same
dose. τ at offset 4.0, with the agent still reaching an objective in 99–100% of episodes in
every row:

| base | pure dim c0 | pure brighten c1 |
|---|---|---|
| s1234 | **−16.5** | +3.0 |
| s9012 | **−25.1** | +2.0 |
| s3456e | **−7.7** | +1.9 |

Matched at first order, identical dose, no loss of competence either way, and a 10–27 step
gap. Brightening is bounded; dimming is not.

**Why, measured inside.** `S_c(g)` is how much of the activation at cell 2 is there because
objective `c` is (erase it and see). Brightening the input saturates the representation,
while dimming has no floor (s9012):

| input gain on c1 | ×2 | ×3 | ×4 | ×8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | 1.62 | 1.90 | 2.01 | 2.41 |

An ×8 input gain buys ×2.4 of internal signal; ×4 buys ×2.0. Dimming colour 0 to ×0.12 takes
S0 to 0.16 with reach still 99.3%. In the competent regime τ is tightly linear in the
internal log signal ratio — brightening: slope −7.0 to −10.1, rms **0.08–0.22 steps** over
6–7 points — so the threshold rule itself is intact; what runs out is how far the
representation can be pushed. Brightening reaches only ln(S1/S0) ≈ 0.85–0.91 before
competence goes, dimming reaches 1.6–2.2.

Caveat: the slope differs by lever (dimming −10.7 to −16.5, rms 0.6–1.6), so this RMS
statistic is a proxy for the decision variable, not the decision variable itself.

**But the axis's mix is not why the axis stalls.** Swapping the axis's gain slice for a
dimming-weighted one barely helps: at offset 4.0 the plain axis reaches −0.9 / −0.6 / −0.7
(reach 11–29%) and the dimming-mix composite −2.8 / −0.6 / −0.6. So the hypothesis as put to
me — that the axis inherits the brightening ceiling *because of its 3.6:1 mix* — is wrong.
**The axis is stopped by the damage its non-input-conv parts do, well before its mix
matters.** The lever asymmetry is real and large; it is just not the axis's binding
constraint.

## 3. The flattening near zero is not selection

τ is fitted on episodes that reached an objective. Recomputed on the levels *every* dose
finished (4,401–8,483 of them), and with the untouched network scored on exactly the levels
each dose finished:

| base | dv 2.5: reach | τ | τ on the common set | base's own τ there (vs overall) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| s1234 | 52% | −0.63 | −0.61 | 6.88 (7.07) |
| s9012 | 29% | −0.21 | −0.14 | 7.18 (7.59) |
| s3456e | 28% | −0.68 | −0.65 | 5.30 (5.30) |

τ and the common-set τ agree within 0.02–0.23 steps at every dose, and the surviving levels
move the base's own threshold by at most 0.4 steps. **The curve keeps its shape.**

Note: the *coarse* interpolation grids in the far-dose runs were decoded with 4,096 episodes
rather than 16,384, and a shorter run is not a prefix of the same level order, so they are
level-paired only among themselves. They are excluded here; the axis and brightness ladders
are unaffected.
